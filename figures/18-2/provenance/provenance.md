## Original source note

`Clark, Loxton, & Tobin 2015. College students (left axis): Revised UCLA Loneliness Scale, trend line across many samples, taken from their fig. 1. High school students (right axis): Mean rating of six loneliness items from the Monitoring the Future survey, triennial means, taken from their fig. 4. Each axis spans half a standard deviation, so the slopes of the college and high school curves are commensurable, but their relative heights are not.`

## Resolved source chain

1. Pinker, *Enlightenment Now*, Figure 18-2.
2. D. Matthew T. Clark, Natalie J. Loxton, and Stephanie J. Tobin, “Declining Loneliness Over Time: Evidence From American Colleges and High Schools,” *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 41(1), 2015, DOI `10.1177/0146167214557007`.
3. College series: 48 American college-student samples using the Revised UCLA Loneliness Scale, analyzed in the paper’s Study 1 and Figure 1.
4. High-school series: Monitoring the Future repeated cross-sectional surveys, six loneliness items, summarized triennially in Study 2 and Figure 4.

The two plotted series are intentionally not placed on a common absolute level. Each axis spans half a standard deviation, and the original paper’s source figures are the immediate numerical source for the displayed trend lines.
